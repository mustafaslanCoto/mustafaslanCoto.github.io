Overview: Emergency Department Delays & Avoidable Harm

Emergency department (ED) crowding and severe inpatient bed exit block create persistent operational bottlenecks in acute hospital systems. In critical stress regimes, extended wait times translate into measurable increases in Estimated Avoidable Deaths (EAD).

Standard linear and single-regime time series models struggle during these episodes because: - Regime Shifts & Asymmetry: Crisis states demonstrate strong persistence (operational lock-in) with dynamics that diverge significantly from baseline conditions. - Heteroskedasticity & Volatility Explosions: Uncertainty and variance expand substantially during peak surge periods.

By formulating the problem as a Markov-Switching AutoRegressive process, the system switches dynamically between Normal (Regime 0 / Teal) and Surge / Peak Risk (Regime 1 / Coral) states, allowing continuous estimation of the latent surge probability \(P(\text{Surge} \mid \text{History})\).

Transition Matrix Dynamics & Regime Persistence

A central property of Markov-Switching models in operational healthcare systems is the transition probability matrix \(A\):

\[ A = \begin{pmatrix} P_{00} & P_{01} \\ P_{10} & P_{11} \end{pmatrix} \]

where: - \(P_{00}\) is the probability that the hospital remains in the Normal baseline regime tomorrow given it is in baseline today. - \(P_{11}\) is the probability that the hospital stays locked in the Surge / Crisis regime tomorrow given it is in crisis today.

Conclusion & Practical Implications

By tracking both the continuous trajectory of avoidable harm and the underlying regime probabilities, healthcare decision makers and bed managers gain: 1. Early Warning Signals: Subtle upward shifts in posterior surge probability \(P(\text{Surge})\) often precede observable bed gridlock by 24–48 hours. 2. Dynamic Uncertainty Bands: Forecasting with regime-specific variance prevents the dangerous underestimation of extreme delay impacts during crisis episodes. 3. Targeted Operational Interventions: Enabling proactive escalation protocols (e.g., rapid cohorting, discharge lounges, step-down coordination) precisely when the system enters high-risk states.
