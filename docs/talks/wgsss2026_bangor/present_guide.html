4. Understand the proposed methodology before editing

My research proposes an interdependent multiseries forecasting framework for forecasting hospital admissions and discharges across multiple specialties at once using shared information between series using single model.

The central idea is to exploit relationships between different time series rather than forecasting every series independently.

The framework supports passing different forecasting models (LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost, Random Forest and Ridge Regression in our experiemens) through a common interface in peshbeen, allowing the same multiseries forecasting approach to be evaluated with different machine-learning and statistical models.

The experiments compare three approaches:

Approach 1: Independent single-series forecasting

Each admissions or discharges series is forecast independently using its own forecasting model and its own historical information.

The results are stored in:

  • point_res_sing: point forecasts for each series using a separate forecasting model for each series.
  • pscore_dict_mod_sing: Pinball Loss scores for probabilistic forecasts from the separate-model approach.

Approach 2: Multiseries forecasting using lags of all series

A forecasting model uses lagged observations from all available admissions and discharges series to forecast the target series (42 days ahead). This allows the model to exploit potentially informative temporal relationships across specialties and between admissions and discharges.

The results are stored in:

  • point_res7: point forecasts for all series using the multiseries approach with lags of all series.
  • pscore_multi_7: Pinball Loss scores for probabilistic forecasts from this approach.

Approach 3: Multiseries forecasting using one selected series per specialty

This approach aims to simplify the input space by using the lagged admissions or discharges series selected for each specialty, rather than using the lags of every available series.

Admissions and discharges are strongly correlated, so the idea is to investigate whether one appropriately selected series per specialty can provide sufficient information to forecast all target series, while reducing model complexity.

The results are stored in:

  • point_res_dict: point forecasts for all series using this reduced-input multiseries approach.
  • pscore_multi_dict: Pinball Loss scores for probabilistic forecasts from this approach.

Inspect the implementation and result structures to confirm these interpretations. In particular, establish how the selected input series are defined and whether the selection is consistent across specialties and experiments.

5. Analyse the new experimental results

Before changing the slides, load and inspect the new results carefully.

Understand the structure of the pickle files, including the series identifiers, model names, forecast horizons, evaluation periods, forecast quantiles, and metric definitions where available.

Do not assume that the files have identical structures or that all models and series are directly comparable. Verify their contents and establish a consistent comparison.

A. Point forecasting

For point forecasting, use RMSSE only in the presentation.

Compare the three approaches:

  1. Independent single-series forecasting.
  2. Multiseries forecasting using lags of all series.
  3. Multiseries forecasting using one selected series per specialty.
  4. Also highlight the winning model among multiseries approaches (LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost, Random Forest, or Ridge Regression).

Evaluate performance across forecasting models and target series, distinguishing admissions from discharges and different specialties where informative.

Use relative performance comparisons where appropriate. For example, calculate the percentage reduction in RMSSE when moving from the single-series baseline to each multiseries approach, provided the comparison is valid.

Investigate whether the performance improvement is consistent across models and series, rather than relying only on averages that may hide substantial variation.

B. Probabilistic forecasting

For probabilistic forecasting, use Pinball Loss only in the presentation.

Compare the corresponding probabilistic forecasting results across the same three approaches, ensuring that quantiles, forecast horizons, evaluation periods, and target series are aligned.

Where the results support it, demonstrate whether the multiseries approaches improve probabilistic forecast quality across the predictive distribution.

Use concise visualisations to show overall performance and meaningful variation across models, series, and quantiles. Avoid overwhelming the audience with every individual score.

C. Forecasting models

The latest experiments include additional models, including CatBoost and random-forecast baselines, alongside the previously evaluated models.

Inspect the actual result files to identify every model that is available and comparable. Include the relevant new models in the presentation.

Do not assume that every model improves under multiseries forecasting. My preliminary impression is that most models perform better under the multiseries approaches, with linear models such as Ridge potentially being exceptions. This is a hypothesis to verify, not a conclusion to reproduce uncritically.

Pay particular attention to:

  • Which models benefit most from cross-series information.
  • Whether nonlinear models benefit more than linear models.
  • Whether the reduced-input multiseries approach retains most of the gains of the full-input approach.
  • Whether the random-forecast baseline provides useful context for interpreting the results.
  • Whether any models or specialties contradict the overall pattern.

Distinguish clearly between observed results and possible explanations for those results.

6. Communicate the research contribution

The presentation should make the central contribution clear:

Can exploiting interdependencies between hospital admissions and discharges across specialties using ONE multiseries forecasting framework improve point and probabilistic forecasting compared with forecasting each series independently?

Use the results to answer this question empirically.

A second important question is whether these benefits can be retained using a simpler multiseries input representation based on one selected series per specialty.

Explain why this matters for healthcare operations. Better forecasts of specialty-level admissions and discharges could support hospital capacity planning, patient-flow management, and the allocation of constrained healthcare resources.

Keep the claims proportional to the evidence. Do not imply that improved forecast accuracy automatically proves better operational outcomes; present operational benefits as potential implications unless they have been directly evaluated.

Also explain the role of peshbeen where useful: it provides a common forecasting interface that enables different forecasting models to be evaluated within the proposed multiseries framework. Make the methodological contribution more prominent than a mere software description.

Create clear, publication-quality visualisations that communicate the results quickly to an interdisciplinary academic audience.

Potential visualisations include:

  • A conceptual diagram showing the three forecasting approaches.
  • A compact comparison of RMSSE across forecasting models.
  • A heatmap showing relative performance improvements across models and target series.
  • A comparison of Pinball Loss across quantiles.
  • A visual comparison of the full-input and reduced-input multiseries approaches.
  • A concise summary of the main empirical findings.

Choose the visualisations that best communicate the actual results. You do not need to include all these options.

Prioritise readable labels, clear legends, consistent terminology, accessible colours, sensible scales, and appropriately sized figures. Use tables only when they communicate the results more clearly than plots.

Ensure that admissions and discharges, model names, and forecasting approaches are consistently labelled throughout the presentation.

Avoid overcrowded slides, excessive decimal places, unnecessary methodological detail, and visualisations that require the audience to inspect many individual numbers to identify the main finding.

Retain useful material from the previous presentation where appropriate, but remove outdated results, redundant explanations, and conclusions that are no longer supported by the latest experiments.
